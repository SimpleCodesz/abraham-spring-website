High-performer burnout coaching · London and online

Burnout coaching for high performers who cannot switch off.

You may still be delivering while the internal cost rises and recovery gives less back. I help high performers examine workload, boundaries, performance identity and practical recovery—without treating coaching as diagnosis or healthcare.

Have a coffee with Abraham › How it works

It's not weakness

When you are what you produce, rest feels like disappearing.

For some high performers, worth and output become closely connected. Slowing down can then feel threatening to identity, status or momentum. That is a pattern to explore, not proof of one neurological cause.

The aim is not to care less about performance. It is to build a foundation underneath it: realistic capacity, better work design, protected recovery and appropriate professional support where health is involved.

How it shows up

If two of these are you, we should talk.

Rest feels like failure

A day off comes with guilt rather than relief, so recovery time is repeatedly filled with more output.

Winning, joylessly

You hit the goal and feel nothing, just the next target. The wins stopped landing a while ago and you have not let yourself notice.

Hiding the empty

Everyone thinks you are thriving. You are managing the gap between how you look and how you feel, and that gap is its own full-time job.

The model stopped working

Push harder, extract more, the formula that built your success is now the thing draining it, and you do not have another one.

The method

Perform from fullness. The R.A.C.E. Framework.

The four-phase method from the book and behind The Unbroken Protocol, applied to a life built on output.

Recover — reduce avoidable load and protect sleep opportunity, food, manageable movement and genuine time away.
Align — reconnect performance to something underneath it, so your worth is not riding on the next result.
Condition — train capacity, not just output, so you can carry load without it carrying you.
Execute — perform from a full tank, with systems that do not depend on you running on empty.

If constant extraction is increasing the cost of performance, more output is unlikely to solve the architecture underneath it. The recovery-led alternative is to rebuild capacity and make future demand more repeatable.

Evidence and boundaries

The work is non-clinical.

The World Health Organization defines burnout as an occupational phenomenon arising from chronic workplace stress that has not been successfully managed. The HSE Management Standards provide a practical way to examine demands, control, support, relationships, role and change. Coaching does not diagnose burnout or replace clinical care.

For the full symptom boundary, read am I burnt out? For an evidence-led comparison when low mood may be involved, read burnout vs depression.

Questions

Before you reach out.

Why can slowing down feel like failing?

When achievement has become closely tied to identity, rest may feel threatening rather than restorative. Coaching can examine that pattern without presenting it as a diagnosis or claiming one universal cause.

I'm still delivering. Could burnout still be relevant?

Output does not always fall first. Persistent work-related exhaustion, growing distance or cynicism and reduced professional effectiveness deserve attention even when some results continue. Those signs are not a diagnosis and may overlap with health conditions.

Is this therapy or coaching?

This is non-clinical coaching, not psychotherapy, diagnosis or treatment. Abraham works with workload, recovery, performance identity and practical systems. Clinical support should take priority when needed.
