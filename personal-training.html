Personal, properly

A programme should adapt to you—not punish you for being human.

Good personal training is not a hard session chosen at random. It starts with where you are, what you want, how you move, what equipment you have and what your week can realistically support.

That means the plan can change when work explodes, sleep falls apart or your body is not absorbing the load. Consistency comes from an intelligent system and a coach who can read the context, not from feeling guilty enough to force another session.

Choose the right service

Personal training or strength and conditioning?

Personal training is the broader route when your priorities are general strength, fitness, movement confidence, body composition or consistency. Strength and conditioning coaching is the more specific route when you need physical preparation for a sport, event or demanding performance goal.

If workload, attention or burnout patterns are what keep destroying consistency, the physical programme can sit inside performance coaching so training and life stop competing with one another.

Still deciding? Use the guides to choosing a personal trainer in London, deciding whether personal training is worth it and selecting how often to see a trainer.

Questions

Personal training, answered.

Where does personal training take place?

In-person personal training is available in London by arrangement. Online coaching is available across the UK and internationally. We agree the practical format during the first conversation.

Is online personal training really personalised?

Yes. Your programme is built around your goals, equipment, schedule, training history and recovery capacity, with ongoing coaching rather than a generic template.

Can you train beginners?

Yes. Training starts from your current capacity and movement confidence. You do not need to get fit before working with a personal trainer.

Can you work around injuries?

Training can be adapted around guidance from an appropriate healthcare professional. Personal training is not diagnosis or treatment, and unexplained or worsening symptoms should be assessed clinically first.
